4. Score header

Sits at the top of the dashboard. The standing message is deliberately vague — never a number, never a rank.

Nothing on the board yet. That is a state, not a verdict.

Top three. Enjoy it — nobody else can see it.

The top three is catchable from here. That is all we are prepared to say.

Your effort is appreciated.

The bar itself comes from scorebar() in src/render.js — this is the real one, not a drawing of it. The whole thing is a link back to the board, and the open-count is what tells a team the starter tiles exist at all.

The four lines are the real band copy from content/economy.js, kept in step by hand — the app emits a bare .standing with no modifier, so the colours are still the kit running ahead of the site. fresh deliberately has no colour of its own: a team on zero has not done anything a colour could report, and the neutral line is the honest one. That leaves .standing--top/--mid/--low for the three bands that are a judgement, which is why four bands still want only three colours.

5. Game tiles — all five states

The dashboard is nothing but a grid of these. Title, state, points. Nothing else.

Every one of these is tile() from src/render.js, the same call the dashboard makes — the state and the title are the only things this page supplies. A locked tile is a <span> and not a link, because there is nowhere to go until you have found it.

6. Hero — two flavours

Top half of every game page. Either words or a picture, never both.

Both are hero() from src/render.js. The kicker is optional and the game page passes none today — no game has a number to put in one — so this page is showing a slot that works rather than one that is used.

9. Form fields

Forms POST and redirect. No client-side validation theatre.

field() from src/render.js, the same call onboarding makes for all nine of its fields. The label wraps the control instead of pointing at it with for — there is no id to keep in sync, which is the one way this markup can break silently, and it breaks by labelling the wrong box.

10. Modal

Fires on every hint reveal, saying a different thing the first time a team ever asks (free, a gift) than every time after (the price, a fine). Built by hintModal() in src/render.js and rendered already open by the server, because the hint has been revealed and charged by the time it appears — it announces, it never asks. On a real page it sits outside .app — because anim-page animates a transform and a transformed ancestor would pin a position: fixed box to the page instead of the viewport — and first, ahead of it, so the box you are looking at is also the first thing read and the first thing tabbed to. That is the whole of this site's focus management (#31, ADR-0015): no trap, no move-in, no restore, no script. Here it is at the end of the page instead, because this is the one place that opens it by hand.

Shown here in its paid framing. The free one is the same box with different words, and there is only one #hint-modal on a page — demoing both would mean a second copy, which is the thing this page no longer keeps.

12. Animations

Three moments. Everything else stays still.

a whole page arriving

That is the whole kit. If a page needs something that is not on this page, the page is wrong or the kit is incomplete — say which.

The scorebar, the tiles, the heroes, the fields, the modal and the window frame above are rendered by src/render.js — the same calls the site makes, so they cannot be out of date. The starburst, the speech bubble, the stamp, the marquee, the status bar and the three standing colours are written by hand here, because no page builds them yet. That list is the design the site still owes. Building one means moving its markup into render.js and leaving a marker here in the same change.
